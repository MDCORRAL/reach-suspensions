## Research Question

Does the racial composition of staff play a more critical role in discipline outcomes in majority-Black environments compared to majority-White environments?

## Hypothesis

The association between % White Teachers and Suspension Rate should be **stronger (steeper slope)** in Q4 (highest % Black students) compared to Q1 (lowest % Black students).

## Interpretation

### Practical Effect Sizes

A **10 percentage point increase** in % White Teachers (e.g., from 40% to 50%) is associated with:

- **Q1 (Lowest % Black)**: +0.116 percentage point change in suspension rate
- **Q2**: +0.057 percentage point change in suspension rate
- **Q3**: +0.140 percentage point change in suspension rate
- **Q4 (Highest % Black)**: +0.371 percentage point change in suspension rate

### Key Finding

The association between teacher racial diversity and suspension rates is **219.8% stronger** in majority-Black schools (Q4) compared to majority-White schools (Q1).

This suggests that teacher racial composition may play a more critical role in discipline outcomes in contexts with higher concentrations of Black students.

---

## Important Caveats

1. **Correlation, not causation**: These are observational patterns in the data, not causal effects.

2. **Confounding variables**: Many unobserved factors influence both teacher diversity and suspension rates:
   - School leadership quality
   - Community demographics and resources
   - Funding levels and resource allocation
   - Local policies and practices
   - Historical and structural context

3. **Ecological analysis**: School-level patterns may not reflect individual-level mechanisms.

4. **Statistical inference**: Formal testing of slope differences would require interaction terms or bootstrapping methods.
